A multiplayer game emits thousands of events a second: players join, score, and win. The leaderboard must update in real time, achievements must fire the moment a streak completes, and analysts want every match in a database.

You build all three on one Redpanda event stream, each as an independent consumer group, so a slow database never delays the leaderboard. The leaderboard itself lives in a compacted topic, with no extra store. You finish with a live dashboard, an achievements feed, and a match history in Postgres, proven by one script.

After completing this solution, you will be able to:

* [ ] Publish Protobuf events with a Schema Registry contract and evolve that contract without breaking a running consumer
* [ ] Fan one topic out to three consumer groups that scale, fail, and replay independently
* [ ] Keep stateful, per-player logic correct across a rebalance by relying on key-based partitioning
* [ ] Materialize a live view in a compacted topic by publishing state instead of deltas, and rebuild it from the log with a group seek
* [ ] Land a stream in Postgres with Redpanda Connect and route every bad record to a dead-letter topic
* [ ] Prove the whole system with exact assertions: counts, sums, lags

== What you build

A game simulator produces `GameEvent` records to two topics. Three consumers read them: a Go leaderboard service that totals each player's score and publishes it to a compacted topic that a small dashboard reads, a Go achievements service that detects streaks and wins and publishes them back to Redpanda, and a Redpanda Connect pipeline that writes match history to Postgres. Redpanda Console shows every record decoded.

* *Game simulator* (Go, franz-go): deterministic event stream, idempotent producer, admin API to burst load or inject a poison record.
* *`game.player-events`* and *`game.match-events`*: the two source topics, keyed by `player_id` and `match_id`.
* *Schema Registry*: holds the `GameEvent` Protobuf schema as subjects `<topic>-value` with `BACKWARD` compatibility.
* *Leaderboard service* (Go): consumer group `leaderboard`, one in-memory total per player, publishes the absolute total to `game.leaderboard` after every score change, scales to three instances.
* *`game.leaderboard`*: a compacted topic keyed by `player_id`. Compaction keeps the newest total per player, so the topic is the leaderboard.
* *Leaderboard dashboard* (Go): reads `game.leaderboard` from the start with no consumer group and serves the live top 10 over server-sent events on a fixed port.
* *Achievements service* (Go): consumer group `achievements`, per-player state kept in memory because every player lives on one partition, produces to `game.achievements`.
* *Redpanda Connect*: consumer group `connect-history`, decodes with Schema Registry, flattens the `oneof`, inserts into Postgres, routes failures to `game.player-events.dlq`.
* *Redpanda Console*: decodes Protobuf records with the same schemas.

== Architecture

image::architecture.svg[Architecture: the simulator produces to two Redpanda topics; the leaderboard service, the achievements service, and Redpanda Connect each read game.player-events in their own consumer group; results land in the compacted topic game.leaderboard, which the dashboard reads, in game.achievements, and in Postgres]

Left to right: the simulator looks the `GameEvent` schema up in Schema Registry (it never registers one; the contract is created in xref:register-schemas.adoc[]) and produces every event about a player to `game.player-events`, keyed by `player_id`, and every match lifecycle event to `game.match-events`, keyed by `match_id`. Three consumer groups read `game.player-events`. The leaderboard group adds each `score_changed` delta to the player's total and publishes that total to `game.leaderboard`, a compacted topic keyed by `player_id`; the dashboard reads that topic from the start and serves the top 10. The achievements group folds each player's events into a small in-memory state and produces `achievement_unlocked` events to `game.achievements`. The `connect-history` group, a Redpanda Connect pipeline, decodes each record through Schema Registry, flattens it, and inserts it into Postgres; a record it cannot decode goes to `game.player-events.dlq` with the error attached. Console reads all five topics with the same schemas.

=== Why Redpanda

* *Independent consumer groups fan one stream out.* Each of the three consumers keeps its own offsets, so scaling the leaderboard, redeploying the achievements service, or a slow Postgres never touches the others. xref:streaming:develop:consume-data/consumer-offsets.adoc[]
* *Per-key ordering.* A record key selects the partition, so every event of one player is delivered in order to exactly one consumer instance. That is what lets the achievements service keep per-player state locally instead of in a shared store. xref:streaming:develop:produce-data/configure-producers.adoc[]
* *Compaction as a materialized view.* A compacted topic keeps the newest record per key, so a service that publishes each player's total turns a topic into the leaderboard itself. Any reader loads the whole board by consuming from the start; no database sits between the stream and the dashboard. xref:streaming:manage:cluster-maintenance/compaction-settings.adoc[]
* *Replay.* Offsets are the consumer's, not the broker's. The replay in xref:scale-and-replay.adoc[] seeks the group to the start, and the service recomputes every total and publishes it again; the compacted topic converges to the same board. xref:streaming:reference:rpk/rpk-group/rpk-group-seek.adoc[]
* *Schema Registry as the contract.* Producers and consumers agree on a Protobuf schema by ID, compatibility rules make evolution safe, and Console and Redpanda Connect decode records with no extra configuration. xref:streaming:manage:schema-reg/schema-reg-overview.adoc[]
* *Redpanda Connect for the sink.* Decode, flatten, insert, dead-letter: fifty lines of YAML, no code. xref:connect:components:outputs/sql_insert.adoc[]

== Prerequisites

* Docker Engine 24 or later with Docker Compose v2, 4 CPUs and 6 GB RAM available to Docker
* `curl` and `make` on the host (every `rpk` and `psql` command runs inside a container)
* Optional: Go 1.25 or later, only if you want to run the unit tests outside Docker
* Free host ports `8080`, `3000`, `3010`, `4195`, `8090`, `15432`, `18081`, `18082`, `19092`, and `19644`, or different ones set with the `*_PORT` variables in `.env` (plus `19000` and `19001` for the optional Tiered Storage extension)
* Optional: a Redpanda Cloud Serverless cluster, if you want to run the same stack against Redpanda Cloud instead of the local broker (xref:start-environment.adoc[] has both paths)
* Approximately {page-solution-duration} minutes

== Production considerations

This solution runs on a single broker with no authentication so that it starts in a minute. Before you reuse the design in production, work through this table.

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
| Area | In this solution | In production

| Brokers and replication
| One broker in `dev-container` mode, replication factor 1.
| Three or more brokers, replication factor 3, rack awareness. xref:streaming:deploy:redpanda/manual/production/production-deployment.adoc[]

| Security
| Plaintext listeners, no authentication, every service is a superuser. The Redpanda Cloud path in xref:start-environment.adoc[] is the exception: TLS and one SCRAM user with ACLs on `game.*`.
| TLS on every listener, SASL/SCRAM or mTLS per service, ACLs that let the simulator write only its two topics and each consumer group read only what it needs. xref:streaming:manage:security/encryption.adoc[], xref:streaming:manage:security/authentication.adoc[], xref:streaming:manage:security/authorization/acl.adoc[]

| Partitions
| `game.player-events` has 6 partitions, `game.leaderboard` 3, the others 3 and 1, sized for a laptop.
| Size for peak throughput and for the parallelism of the largest consumer group; the partition count is the ceiling on consumer instances. xref:streaming:deploy:redpanda/manual/sizing.adoc[]

| Schema validation
| Schemas are registered and consumers reject unknown IDs, but the broker accepts any bytes (xref:persist-match-history.adoc[] proves it with a poison record).
| Enable schema ID validation on `game.player-events` and `game.match-events` so the broker rejects a record whose schema ID is not registered under the topic's subject. xref:streaming:manage:schema-reg/schema-id-validation.adoc[]

| Consumer scaling
| One instance per group; xref:scale-and-replay.adoc[] scales the leaderboard to three by hand.
| Scale on consumer group lag, keep instances at or below the partition count, and use static group membership for stateful services so a restart does not trigger a rebalance. xref:streaming:develop:consume-data/consumer-offsets.adoc[]

| Data retention
| `game.player-events` keeps 7 days on local disk; the other event topics use the cluster default. xref:index.adoc#_extend_this_solution[Extend this solution] switches Tiered Storage on with MinIO.
| Tiered Storage: long retention and replay from object storage without growing local disks. xref:streaming:manage:tiered-storage.adoc[]

| Compaction of `game.leaderboard`
| `cleanup.policy=compact` with `segment.ms=60000` (and the cluster floor `log_segment_ms_min` lowered to match) so compaction is visible within minutes. Compaction runs every 10 seconds on closed segments.
| Keep `compact`, but size `segment.ms` and `min.cleanable.dirty.ratio` for the write rate: rolling a segment a minute is churn, not a requirement. Compaction is best effort, so readers keep the last record per key rather than assuming one per key. xref:streaming:manage:cluster-maintenance/compaction-settings.adoc[]

| Idempotency and delivery
| Idempotent producer with `acks=all`; consumers commit after writing, so a crash can replay a batch. Postgres inserts are `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`. The leaderboard publishes absolute totals, each stamped with the source offset it includes, so a replayed record is recognized and republishing a total is harmless (xref:build-leaderboard.adoc[]).
| Publish state, not deltas, wherever a consumer materializes a view, and record the source position in it; use transactions where a consumer also produces and the output must be exactly-once. xref:streaming:develop:produce-data/idempotent-producers.adoc[], xref:streaming:develop:transactions.adoc[]

| Dead-letter handling
| Redpanda Connect routes undecodable records to `game.player-events.dlq` with the error as a header. The Go services log and skip them.
| Alert on dead-letter topic growth, keep the original key and headers so records can be replayed after a fix, and give the DLQ its own retention. xref:connect:configuration:error_handling.adoc[]

| Observability
| Redpanda Console, the dashboard's lag and `records read` counters, and `rpk group describe`.
| Prometheus metrics from the brokers and from Redpanda Connect, dashboards, alerts on consumer lag and under-replicated partitions. xref:streaming:manage:monitoring.adoc[], xref:connect:guides:monitoring.adoc[]

| Disaster recovery
| None.
| Shadowing replicates topics, consumer offsets, and schemas to a second cluster so the leaderboard can fail over. xref:streaming:manage:disaster-recovery/shadowing/overview.adoc[]

| Analytics and lakehouse
| Postgres holds the match history for queries.
| Iceberg topics write `game.match-events` and `game.player-events` straight to a lakehouse table for analysts, with no pipeline to run. xref:streaming:manage:iceberg/about-iceberg-topics.adoc[]
|===

=== Keep a season of events with Tiered Storage

`game.player-events` keeps seven days on local disk. A season of a live game is months of events, and the replay in xref:scale-and-replay.adoc[] is only as deep as the retention. Tiered Storage moves closed segments to object storage and reads them back on demand, so retention becomes a setting instead of a disk size. Tiered Storage is an enterprise feature.
