= Price the odds
:page-layout: solution-step
:description: Turn provider probabilities into prices in the first of three independent consumer groups, and measure the end-to-end latency from the records themselves.
:page-solution-step-duration: 6

The odds engine is the first of the three consumers. It reads `sports.feed` in the group `odds-engine`, prices every market update, and publishes the result to `sports.odds`. It also refuses to publish three kinds of price, and the reason it counts each refusal separately is the point of this step.

== Why

The engine publishes a price only for a market update with a selection, and it publishes nothing at all for a market it believes is suspended. Those are different outcomes with different meanings, so they are different counters: `skipped` is "that event was never about a price", and `held_suspended` is "the market is closed, the last price stands". A single "ignored" counter would hide the second one, and a suspended market quietly publishing prices is how a book takes a bet it cannot honour.

The third refusal is the interesting one. A probability of zero, or one, or something that implies a price below the book's floor, is a provider error, and the engine counts it as `rejected` and publishes nothing. A wrong price on every screen is worse than no new price, and the feed's own bounds are checked by a unit test so that a rejection means the provider misbehaved rather than the generator drifting:

[source,go]
----
include::example$services/odds/pricing/pricing.go[tag=price]
----

Offsets are committed by hand, after the prices of a batch have been acknowledged by the broker. A crash between publishing and committing replays the batch, and a replayed price is the same price: the record is keyed by market and carries the sequence it came from, so a duplicate changes nothing.

== Why Redpanda

The engine is a member of a consumer group, so Redpanda assigns it partitions, tracks its offsets, and rebalances when instances come and go. Run three copies with `docker compose up -d --scale odds-engine=3` and the six partitions split between them; each instance then owns the fixtures on its partitions and nobody else's, which is why per-market suspension state can live in memory. Committing offsets manually is what turns "at least once" into something a consumer can reason about. See xref:streaming:develop:consume-data/consumer-offsets.adoc[].

== In production

[.production-note]
Pricing is a trading decision, not a formula, and the overround here is a placeholder for one. What is not a placeholder is the accounting: every event in exactly one bucket, and no price published for a market you believe is closed. See the Latency budget row in xref:index.adoc#_production_considerations[Production considerations].
