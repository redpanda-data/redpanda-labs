Then you look at where those records landed, because the partition a record goes to is the only reason the three consumers in the next steps can be as simple as they are.

== Why

The producer sets three things that are not defaults, and each one is the answer to a way feeds go wrong.

The key is the fixture. A sportsbook does not need a global order across every match in the world, and asking for one would mean one partition and no parallelism. It does need a strict order within a match, because a suspension that arrives after the price it was meant to suspend is a market open at a price the book did not intend. Keying by fixture buys exactly that guarantee, and nothing more.

The producer waits for `acks=all` on every event, one at a time. A feed that reports success before the cluster has durably stored the event is a feed that loses events in precisely the conditions that matter, when a spike is in progress and something is restarting. Throughput here comes from the partition count, not from cutting the acknowledgement.

The provider's sequence number is carried in the event rather than derived from the offset. The offset is Redpanda's count of what it received; the sequence is the provider's count of what it sent. Keeping both is how you tell "we dropped it" from "they never sent it", and that difference ends up in a commercial conversation.

The `event_type` also goes in a record header, so a consumer can route on it without decoding the body.

== Why Redpanda

The record key selects the partition, and the partition is the unit of ordering and of consumer parallelism: one partition is read by exactly one member of a group at a time. That is what makes per-key ordering and horizontal scaling the same mechanism rather than a trade-off. With `acks=all` the producer waits for every in-sync replica, which on a three-broker cluster means the event survives a broker loss. See xref:streaming:develop:produce-data/configure-producers.adoc[] and xref:streaming:develop:produce-data/idempotent-producers.adoc[].

== In production

[.production-note]
A real provider feed arrives over the provider's own transport, and the gateway's job is to get it onto the log unchanged, with the provider's timestamps and sequence numbers intact. See the Partitions and Feed quality rows in xref:index.adoc#_production_considerations[Production considerations].
